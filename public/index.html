What you're looking at, and what's still wrong with it

The invented words come from a character n-gram trained on SCOWL's 127k-word English lexicon, with everything that's already a word in English or 41 other languages thrown out, plus anything unpronounceable, anything on a slur/profanity blocklist, and anything too close to the word it replaces. Words are matched by length and assigned in frequency order, so English-ish's commonest words are its most ordinary-looking ones.

Morphology is mirrored, not regularised. If you know a stem you get its whole family — and irregularity is preserved by class, not by word. English's sing/sang/sung, ring/rang/rung and swim/swam/swum all share one vowel alternation because they're the residue of Proto-Germanic ablaut; English-ish gives that class its own alternation and applies it to the same members. So English-ish has ablaut, umlaut, zero-marked pasts and suppletion, in the same places English does. Paradigms are checked to collapse exactly where English's collapse: walk/walked/walked merges, sing/sang/sung doesn't.

Known problems, honestly:

  • Stems still contain Latinate affix debris — things that read like -tion or -ility wearing a hat. Fixing it needs a morphological database that knows bound stems (spect, struct, ceive aren't words), because no "strip it if the remainder is a word" rule can reach them.
  • Candidates are ranked by model probability, which explains only ~16–30% of how word-like humans actually judge a nonword. Neighbourhood density would be the better key.
  • Homographs collapse: saw is both see.PAST and a tool, and the commoner one wins. That needs a part-of-speech tagger.
  • Short words are the weakest link: there are only 33 two-letter strings and ~2,700 three-letter ones that aren't already English or a common foreign word, so the commonest words draw from a very thin pool.

The coverage/comprehension gap is the real point. Going from 1,000 to 2,000 words buys the biggest jump in coverage (+9 points) and the smallest in comprehension (+7). Going 5,000→7,000 buys only +1.3 coverage but +17 comprehension. Coverage looks reassuring long before reading does. This is Hu & Nation's 2000 experiment — they replaced rare words with nonsense to force a coverage level, on 66 students. It failed to replicate in 2023.
